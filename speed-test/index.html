📊 Understanding Your Results

How Are Speed Metrics Measured?

  • Download: The rate at which data transfers from the web server to your device. Measured in Megabits per second (Mbps). Essential for streaming Netflix/YouTube, browsing, and downloading files.
  • Upload: The speed at which your device sends data to the cloud. Crucial for video conferencing (Zoom/Teams), security camera feeds, and uploading videos.
  • Ping (RTT): Round-trip latency in milliseconds (ms). Represents the time it takes for a data packet to reach the destination and bounce back.
  • Jitter: Variance in ping latency over time. Low jitter (< 5ms) ensures smooth real-time voice and multiplayer gaming without rubberbanding.
🛠️ Router Optimization Tips

How to Boost Your Wi-Fi & Internet Speed

  1. Switch to 5GHz or 6GHz Wi-Fi Band: The 5GHz frequency delivers significantly higher throughput and less interference than the congested 2.4GHz band.
  2. Relocate Your Router: Place your wireless gateway in an elevated, central location away from thick concrete walls, microwaves, and metal enclosures.
  3. Change Wi-Fi Channels: Log into your router admin portal (e.g. 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1) and switch to uncongested channels (1, 6, 11 for 2.4GHz; 36, 44, 149 for 5GHz).
  4. Enable QoS (Quality of Service): Prioritize work devices or gaming consoles over background cloud backups to eliminate bufferbloat.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Mbps and MB/s?

Mbps stands for Megabits per second (used by ISPs and speed tests to measure bandwidth), while MB/s stands for Megabytes per second (used for file download size). There are 8 bits in 1 byte, so a 100 Mbps connection downloads files at approximately 12.5 MB/s.

What is considered a good ping and jitter for gaming?

A ping under 20ms is exceptional for competitive gaming. 20ms to 50ms is good, while anything above 100ms causes noticeable lag. Jitter measures ping variance; an ideal jitter score is below 5ms.

Why is my Wi-Fi speed slower than my router's advertised speed?

Advertised speeds (e.g. AX3000 or AC1200) are theoretical physical link rates under laboratory conditions. Real-world speeds are affected by distance, wall interference, 2.4GHz vs 5GHz channel congestion, and client device antenna limitations.
